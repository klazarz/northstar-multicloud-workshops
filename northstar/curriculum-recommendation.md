# Oracle Autonomous AI Database Serverless on Azure: proposed LiveLabs curriculum

Research snapshot: 27 November 2026. This is a curriculum design, not a validated workshop or a confirmed LiveLabs sandbox offering. Times are estimates to be measured in pilots. The local documentation index remains the scope reference.

## Recommendation

Build a 45-minute QuickStart and six reusable 90-minute workshops. Offer two three-workshop paths: one for day-to-day operations and one for resilience and governance. Publish the QuickStart and workshops 1–3 first; develop workshops 4–6 once the necessary environments and permissions are proven. Each workshop ends in a visible deliverable and can also start from a prepared environment.

**The story:** Northstar Outfitters, a fictional outdoor retailer, wants reliable inventory information for a seasonal promotion. Maya, the business operations lead, needs an answer she can act on; Alex, the cloud database specialist, helps the learner deliver it. The learner is the hands-on teammate, making decisions and showing Maya evidence. Their business challenge evolves from establishing a trusted inventory snapshot to keeping it observable, protected, and recoverable. Characters introduce context and react to outcomes briefly; they never replace instructions.

**QuickStart — A trustworthy answer before the promotion** (beginner, 45 minutes): With an environment ready, connect to an existing database, load a small provided inventory dataset, run a query identifying low-stock items, and give Maya a result she can use. Timeline: story and orientation 5; connect 10; load or execute supplied data setup 10; query and interpret 15; explain result and next steps 5. Success: a query returns the expected low-stock products and the learner can explain the result. This is a meaningful first win independent of provisioning. For an Azure-provided sandbox, access and SQL tooling must first be verified; for bring-your-own, complete the environment checklist in advance. Connection options depend on network mode; Oracle documents a client connection through an Azure VM with network security prerequisites ([connection guide](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azucn-connect-c-autonomous-ai-database-serverless.html)).

## Learning paths

| Path | Workshop 1 | Workshop 2 | Workshop 3 | End-of-day achievement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Operate with confidence** | 1. Launch the inventory service (beginner) | 2. See and scale the service (intermediate) | 3. Recover from a mistake (intermediate) | A running, observable inventory database with a demonstrated recovery plan |
| **Protect the business** | 1. Launch the inventory service (beginner) | 4. Secure the inventory service (intermediate) | 5. Design for disruption (advanced) | An evidenced security configuration and a continuity decision with a rehearsed response |

Workshop 6, **Follow the signals**, is a specialist advanced elective and can replace workshop 2 in the first path for teams focused on integration, provided its additional setup is ready. It is not part of the core first release.

**Day schedule:** QuickStart 09:00–09:45; workshop 1 09:45–11:15; break 11:15–11:30; workshop 2 11:30–13:00; lunch 13:00–13:45; workshop 3 13:45–15:15; contingency and discussion 15:15–15:45; optional cleanup 15:45–16:15. That is 5 hours 15 minutes of learning plus 30 minutes contingency, 60 minutes breaks, and up to 30 minutes cleanup: 7 hours 15 minutes total. Account preparation, subscriptions, entitlement, and any long-running provisioning are completed before 09:00. Provisioning and cleanup may exceed these estimates; the facilitator must measure them in each environment. If workshops run as separate sessions, provide a prepared equivalent database and dataset and a short checkpoint rather than requiring earlier workshops.

## Workshop catalog

All workshops assume the QuickStart's basic connection and data skills. Workshop 1 includes a provisioned fallback so that waiting does not prevent completion. The time allocations below sum to 90 minutes and include validation and wrap-up. All detailed exercises and durations require live pilot validation.

### 1. Launch the inventory service — beginner, priority 1

**Audience:** cloud newcomers, Azure practitioners, DBAs, and application owners. **Challenge:** Maya needs a reliable inventory snapshot before the promotion; Alex asks the learner to create a database and make it usable from Azure. **Objectives:** explain the Azure and Oracle roles; identify a delegated subnet and access path; provision a database; connect and verify sample data.

**Sequence:** story and architecture 10 min; inspect Azure access, network and subnet 15; choose configuration and initiate creation 20; while waiting inspect lifecycle and connection options 10; connect to the newly created database or prepared fallback and load the sample 20; show the low-stock result and explain the environment 10; wrap-up 5. **Finish:** database status and successful query are the handoff to Maya. **Standalone:** provide ready access, a prepared subnet, SQL tool and dataset; creation is hands-on where permissions allow. **Constraints:** database creation and network permissions, quotas, regional availability, cost, and elapsed provisioning time must be confirmed. If fallback is used, report that the learner observed provisioning rather than completed it. [Create subnet](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azucr-create-delegated-subnet.html), [create database](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azucr-create-autonomous-ai-database-serverless.html), [connect](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azucn-connect-c-autonomous-ai-database-serverless.html).

### 2. See and scale the service — intermediate, priority 1

**Audience:** Azure operators, DBAs, application owners. **Challenge:** Maya expects a spike in demand and wants a credible capacity and cost decision. **Objectives:** inspect monitoring, interpret baseline workload signals, inspect compute and storage settings, explain auto scaling and the cost tradeoff.

**Sequence:** business scenario 5; inspect baseline settings and status 15; explore Azure Monitor metrics 20; execute a bounded sample workload and compare observations 15; inspect or adjust an approved scaling setting 15; create a short capacity and cost recommendation 15; wrap-up 5. **Finish:** a baseline observation and a recommended setting with its rationale. A lack of visible autoscaling is a valid result; do not claim scaling occurred without evidence. **Standalone:** prepared database, dataset, monitoring access and known baseline. **Constraints:** avoid promising a meaningful workload spike or immediate telemetry; provision examples in advance if needed. Oracle's [create feature matrix](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azucr-create.html) lists compute and storage auto scaling options; [database modification](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azumd-modify-autonomous-ai-database-serverless.html), [Azure Monitor](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azumn-monitor-azure-monitor.html), and [billing](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azumn-monitor-billing.html) supply the operational context. Performance optimization beyond basic capacity decisions needs further source and workload validation.

### 3. Recover from a mistake — intermediate, priority 1

**Audience:** DBAs, operations engineers, incident responders. **Challenge:** an incorrect inventory update threatens the promotion. **Objectives:** inspect automatic backups; compare backup and point-in-time recovery; recover a disposable copy and verify a known marker; explain retention choices.

**Sequence:** incident and recovery choices 10; inspect backup and retention 15; establish a known data marker and recovery target 15; initiate a supported recovery or clone of a disposable database 15; inspect progress and practice the response plan while waiting 15; verify recovered marker on a completed prepared recovery if necessary 15; handoff 5. **Finish:** a recovery evidence record showing the target and verified result, identifying any prepared fallback. **Standalone:** seeded database and an available recovery point or pre-created recovery; timestamps and readiness must be established before the session. **Constraints:** never restore over the shared primary; actual restore and backup availability may exceed the session. Explain automatic, manual, and long-term retention using the [automatic](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azubu-backup-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-automatic.html), [manual](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azubu-backup-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-manual.html), [long-term retention](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azubu-backup-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-ltr.html), [restore](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azurs-restore-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-restore.html), [point-in-time restore](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azurs-restore-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-pitr.html), and [clone](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azurs-restore-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-clone.html) guides. Validate exact supported recovery method before authoring instructions.

### 4. Secure the inventory service — intermediate, priority 2

**Audience:** security engineers, Azure administrators, DBAs, governance teams. **Challenge:** Maya needs to share inventory data while protecting customer and business information. **Objectives:** trace the connection path, identify transport and storage encryption, compare key ownership approaches, and produce an evidence-based security handoff.

**Sequence:** scenario and threat boundaries 10; inspect network and connection settings 20; verify a supported encrypted connection and inspect the appropriate wallet or client settings 20; inspect encryption and compare provider-managed and customer-managed key options 20; review compliance source and produce a handoff 15; wrap-up 5. **Finish:** a connection check and security decision record grounded in observed settings. **Standalone:** preconfigured connection and access to relevant settings; key-management setup is optional and requires a separately prepared environment. **Constraints:** do not rotate, replace or revoke shared keys as a workshop exercise. Explain that a wallet used for client connectivity and encryption key management serve distinct purposes; inspect the exact context of each feature. [In-transit encryption](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azusr-security-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-transit.html), [Oracle Wallet](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azusr-security-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-oracle-wallet.html), [OCI Vault](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azusr-security-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-oci-vault.html), [Azure Key Vault](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azusr-security-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-akv.html), and [compliance](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azusr-security-compliance.html).

### 5. Design for disruption — advanced, priority 2

**Audience:** architects, DBAs, reliability and business continuity teams. **Challenge:** Maya asks what happens if the primary location becomes unavailable. **Objectives:** translate a scenario into recovery objectives; compare cross-AZ and cross-region approaches; inspect a prepared protection configuration; document and rehearse a role-transition decision.

**Sequence:** impact and objectives 10; inspect current database and protection state 15; compare cross-AZ and cross-region designs 20; inspect a preconfigured peer and its prerequisites 15; tabletop switchover and validation checklist 20; business handoff 10. **Finish:** an actionable continuity decision and a completed rehearsal record. **Standalone:** primary, architecture diagram and prepared peer evidence; an actual peer or switchover is an optional isolated lab, conditional on verified environment and time. **Constraints:** cross-region setup requires a supported peer region, a private virtual network deployment and suitable target networking among other prerequisites. Actual transition must never disrupt the shared primary. [Cross-AZ](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azubc-business-continuity-hadr-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-cross-az.html), [cross-region](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azubc-business-continuity-hadr-autonomous-ai-database-serverless-cross-region.html), and [capability matrix](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azubc-business-continuity-hadr.html).

### 6. Follow the signals — advanced elective, priority 3

**Audience:** cloud platform engineers, observability and security operations. **Challenge:** Maya wants the right team to see evidence of issues before customer orders are affected. **Objectives:** trace available telemetry, inspect an Azure diagnostic destination, query available records, and explain when Event Hubs or OCI monitoring fits.

**Sequence:** incident and signal map 10; inspect Azure Monitor and diagnostic settings 20; inspect a prepared Log Analytics workspace and query available records 20; examine an Event Hubs routing design or prepared configuration 15; compare OCI-native monitoring 15; deliver signal ownership and escalation summary 10. **Finish:** an observed signal or explicitly identified absence, plus a routing and ownership map. **Standalone:** prepared diagnostic settings, workspace, representative records, permissions, and optional Event Hubs access. **Constraints:** telemetry availability and ingestion latency require pilot validation; Event Hubs delivery is a demonstration unless provisioned and verified. [Azure Monitor](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azumn-monitor-azure-monitor.html), [Log Analytics](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azumn-monitor-azure-log-analytics.html), [Event Hubs](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azumn-monitor-azure-event-hub.html), [OCI monitoring](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azumn-monitor-oci.html).

## Coverage and environment

| Indexed topic | Coverage | Delivery |
|---|---|---|
| Create subnet and database; connect | QuickStart, 1 | Hands-on, with prepared fallback |
| Modify subnet and database | 1, 2, 4 | Inspect subnet configuration; change database settings where permitted. Subnet modification is a guided comparison, not a shared-network mutation. [Subnet modification](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azumd-modify-delegated-subnet.html) |
| Scaling and optimization | 2 | Capacity and auto scaling; deeper optimization remains a research gap |
| Automatic, manual, long-term backups; restore, point-in-time restore, clone | 3 | Inspect and recover disposable copy where feasible; long-term retention and manual backup are decisions or demonstration unless time and access permit |
| Cross-AZ and cross-region | 5 | Design and tabletop; optional isolated live operation |
| Azure Monitor, Event Hubs, Log Analytics, OCI monitoring | 2, 6 | Monitor and inspect prepared telemetry; routing demonstration if needed |
| Encryption in transit, wallet, OCI Vault, Azure Key Vault | 4 | Verify connection and inspect settings; key administration optional |
| Billing and compliance | 2, 4 | Cost decision and evidence review, not a compliance certification |
| Delete database and delegated subnet | Final cleanup | Only learner-owned resources, after all path workshops; follow [database deletion](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azudl-delete-autonomous-ai-database-serverless.html) and [subnet deletion](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/azudl-delete-delegated-subnet.html) prerequisites. Never delete shared infrastructure. |

**Bring your own:** Before the event, confirm Azure subscription and Oracle AI Database@Azure entitlement, account linking and roles, regional availability, quota, network, client access, budget and cleanup ownership. Provide explicit per-workshop permissions and an estimated cost after choosing actual settings and regional pricing. Preserve the primary and sample data between sessions; isolate restores, clones and any destructive activity. Provide a checkpoint script or prepared dataset for standalone entry, validated before release.

**Provided sandbox:** LiveLabs describes sandbox reservations generally ([LiveLabs repository](https://github.com/oracle-livelabs/developer)), but this research did not establish a sandbox that provides this particular Azure service, linked accounts, cross-region access, required roles, or sufficient reservation time. Treat it as a delivery design to validate with the LiveLabs sandbox and service teams, not an available offering. A suitable sandbox needs per-learner isolation or carefully managed shared resources, both cloud identities, network access, starter data, optional recovery and telemetry fixtures, lifecycle ownership, reset and cleanup. If unavailable, offer bring-your-own and facilitated demonstrations until an appropriate environment exists.

## Existing material and authoring

Oracle's [Autonomous Database training plans](https://www.oracle.com/database/technologies/datawarehouse-bigdata/adw-training-plans.html) and [database workshops](https://oracle-livelabs.github.io/database/) provide established Autonomous Database learning material; an [Oracle 90-minute data workshop description](https://www.oracle.com/developer/day-one-and-beyond/database/) covers provisioning, loading and analysis. These are relevant sources for reuse of general concepts and data exercises. Their descriptions do not establish an equivalent end-to-end Autonomous Serverless on Azure operations curriculum or sandbox; catalog coverage and current workshop availability need a deeper LiveLabs catalog review before claiming novelty.

Public Oracle LiveLabs repositories provide examples and common material, including [common resources](https://github.com/oracle-livelabs/common). Current authoritative internal authoring, manifest, accessibility, editorial and publishing rules were not established by this research. Use verified current templates and have the LiveLabs publishing team review them before authoring; do not infer mandatory syntax from examples.

## Quality gate

Proposed rubric, subject to approval: eight equally weighted criteria, each scored 0–5: measurable outcome and proof; story and learner motivation; technical accuracy; feasibility and environment; realistic timing; accessibility and clarity; LiveLabs conformance; standalone completion. Overall score is the arithmetic mean. Require at least 4.5 overall and no criterion below 4.0, with no unresolved critical accuracy, accessibility or execution defect. Score each workshop individually, including QuickStart. A reviewer independent of the author assesses source evidence, rendered instructions and observed pilot execution for both delivery options where available; a representative learner pilot measures timing, comprehension and completion. Record evidence, defects, fixes and reassessment. No workshop currently has a verified score: these are design proposals, not independently reviewed or live-tested workshops.

1. Confirm the intended audience and priority of the two proposed paths after reviewing the curriculum.
2. Confirm whether an Azure-capable LiveLabs sandbox can be supplied, its capabilities, session duration and cost owner.
3. Obtain current LiveLabs authoring and review requirements, and agree the rubric and independent reviewers.
4. Pilot provisioning, connection, telemetry, recovery and cleanup; record actual wait times, costs and permissions. Verify the individual source pages and supported operation for every exercise when authoring.
5. Choose a supported region and design actual recovery fixtures before promising hands-on cross-region protection or a complete 90-minute recovery exercise.
